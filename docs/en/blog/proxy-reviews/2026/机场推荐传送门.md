The source described these as high-speed nodes in mainstream countries. An Anycast label does not guarantee which server handles a connection, the physical egress location, or equal performance from every ISP.

## Selecting a plan

- Light use: compare Traveler with the annual small plan, but note the annual prepayment.
- Regular video and work: test Explorer or the monthly 400 GB plan.
- High-frequency use: do not interpret "unlimited" as absence of an acceptable-use rule.
- Irregular long-term use: one-time data still depends on provider continuity.
- Business or livestream use: purchase the dedicated route only after verifying country, IP type, exclusivity, replacement, reputation, and support terms.

## Risks to resolve

- Are the 500 Mbps, 1,000 Mbps, 2,000 Mbps, and 5,000 Mbps values node caps or shared route capacity?
- What exact conduct triggers the unlimited-plan suspension?
- Do "permanent" packages expire after inactivity or account changes?
- Which devices and operating-system versions receive provider-app updates?
- Can the subscription be exported to a trusted third-party client?
- What written service terms apply to the dedicated route?

## Use process

1. Open the [recorded registration route](https://aa.csmfby.com/?code=b9eUUu00).
2. Verify the app publisher, signature, permissions, and update source.
3. Buy the smallest monthly tier rather than a permanent package.
4. Test evening-peak stability, automatic switching, recovery, and support for one or two weeks.
5. Increase the commitment only when measured use justifies it.

## FAQ

### Is Portal beginner-friendly?

Its provider app may reduce configuration work. The tradeoff is dependence on that app and its maintenance.

### Which plan is a reasonable starting point?

Traveler or another small monthly tier. The CNY 99 headline option is an annual payment, not a monthly trial.

### What needs the most scrutiny?

The app's platform support and permissions, unlimited-plan suspension rule, and the lifetime meaning of one-time packages.
