## What are VPNs and China-focused proxy services?

::: collapse
- VPNs and proxy services: differences and selection advice

  A **VPN, or virtual private network**, is an encrypted network connection that can protect data in transit. A China-focused **proxy service**, colloquially called an “airport” in Chinese, usually provides access through protocols such as Shadowsocks, V2Ray, or Trojan.

  **Main differences:**

  - **VPN:** Commercial VPN products are usually integrated apps, often cost more (roughly CNY 20–80 per month in the source market), and offer broad platform support. They may suit users who prioritize a single packaged product.
  - **Proxy service:** Plans are often less expensive (entry-level plans commonly cost about CNY 3–15 per month), focus on circumventing network restrictions, and support flexible rule-based routing through third-party clients.

  **How should you choose?**

  1. **Stability:** Prefer providers that have operated for at least two years to reduce shutdown risk.
  2. **Speed:** Providers with IEPL or IPLC private routes may offer better peak-hour performance; verify with your own connection before committing.
  3. **Price:** Match the plan to actual data use. Light users can consider a plan below CNY 10 or a non-expiring data package.
  4. **Payment:** Confirm that the provider accepts a payment method available to you.
  5. **Support:** Look for an active [Telegram](/en/blog/telegram/) group or a functioning ticket system.

- Standards for inexpensive plans

  Value for money matters in these reviews. Low-cost plans, packages below CNY 10, and non-expiring data packages have a low entry cost and can work for trials or backup access.

  The screening criteria used here include:

  - ✅ clearly stated prices and no undisclosed fees;
  - ✅ nodes that do not disconnect repeatedly;
  - ✅ mainstream client support, including [Clash](/en/article/0gematwc/), V2Ray, and [Shadowrocket](/en/article/z747kgjd/);
  - ✅ the claimed ability to access services such as Netflix, ChatGPT, and YouTube, subject to current node testing;
  - ✅ a trial or stated refund policy where noted.

- Common questions

  **Q1: Are VPNs and proxy services safe?**

  **A:** Risk depends on the provider, jurisdiction, configuration, and what the connection is used for. Practical precautions include:

  - avoid unknown free VPNs that do not clearly explain data handling;
  - prefer providers with a longer operating history and a verifiable reputation;
  - do not treat a proxy provider as a trusted endpoint for sensitive plaintext data.

  **Q2: How much monthly data do I need?**

  **A:** Approximate ranges by use case:

  - light use (web browsing and social platforms): 50–100 GB/month;
  - moderate use (video and downloads): 200–500 GB/month;
  - heavy use (4K video and large downloads): 500 GB–1 TB/month.

  **Q3: How do I use a proxy service?**

  **A:** The basic process is:

  1. register and purchase a plan;
  2. install a client such as Clash, V2Ray, or Shadowrocket;
  3. copy the subscription URL and import it into the client;
  4. choose a node and connect.

  **Q4: Which devices are supported?**

  **A:** Mainstream providers commonly support:

  - Windows, macOS, and Linux computers;
  - iOS and Android phones;
  - [routers](/en/blog/router-setup-guide/), including the [beginner OpenWrt router guide](/en/blog/router-setup-guide/);
  - Apple TV, game consoles, and other devices when configured through a compatible client or router.

- Access-tool trends in 2026

  1. **New protocols:** Hysteria and TUIC aim to improve performance and resistance to disruption.
  2. **Lower entry prices:** More inexpensive and pay-as-you-go packages have appeared.
  3. **More private routes:** IEPL and IPLC routes are becoming more common among midrange and premium providers.
  4. **AI-service routing:** More providers claim to optimize access to ChatGPT, Claude, and related services; verify current availability on the specific node you will use.

### Step 3: import a subscription and connect

1. Copy the subscription URL from the provider's website.
2. Open the client and find the subscription or profile section.
3. Paste the URL and update the profile.
4. Choose a suitable node; lower latency is often a useful starting point.
5. Enable rule-based or global proxy mode as needed.

**Proxy modes:**

- **Rule mode:** Routes selected traffic through the proxy and sends other traffic directly. This usually saves data and is the default recommendation.
- **Global mode:** Sends all supported traffic through the proxy. Use it when a site or app is not matched by existing rules.
- **Direct mode:** Does not use the proxy.

### Step 4: test the connection

After connecting, test several independent services:

- [Google](https://google.com) — basic connectivity;
- [YouTube](https://youtube.com) — sustained video throughput;
- [Netflix](https://netflix.com) — regional streaming availability;
- [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com) — AI-service access from the selected exit IP.

## Security and privacy guidance

### Practical privacy precautions

1. **Be cautious with free VPNs**
   - Unknown free services may have unclear security and data-handling practices.
   - Some may monetize activity or device data.
   - Free capacity is often slow or unstable.

2. **Choose a provider with verifiable operations**
   - Prefer a provider with at least two years of history.
   - Review public user feedback and unresolved disputes.
   - Avoid providers with no group, support channel, or operating record.

3. **Protect account credentials**
   - Use a unique password that is not reused on other sites.
   - Enable two-factor authentication when available.
   - Change credentials if the provider reports a breach or data loss.

4. **Use the service responsibly**
   - Follow applicable local laws and regulations.
   - Do not use the service for illegal activity.
   - Remember that the provider may be able to observe connection metadata and any unencrypted traffic.

## Troubleshooting

### No internet access after connecting

- Check that the subscription URL was imported correctly.
- Try another node.
- Confirm the selected proxy mode.
- Verify that the underlying local network works without the proxy.

### Slow connection

- Select a lower-latency node.
- Compare performance outside the common 8–11 p.m. peak period.
- Check whether the plan's data limit has been exceeded.
- Test an IEPL route before paying to upgrade.

### A specific website does not open

- Switch nodes because the current exit IP may be blocked.
- Try global mode to rule out an incomplete routing rule.
- Check whether the site applies regional restrictions.
- Ask the provider whether it currently supports that service.

### Frequent disconnections

- Check the stability of the local connection first.
- Test another supported protocol, such as moving from Shadowsocks to V2Ray or Trojan.
- Avoid an overloaded node.
- Compare against a backup provider before concluding that the client is at fault.

## Frequently asked questions

### How should I choose a more reliable proxy service in 2026?

Prioritize providers with a longer operating history, standard subscription support, reachable ticket or group support, stable peak-hour performance, and payment methods you can use. Start with a trial or monthly plan, then test YouTube, ChatGPT, Google, and GitHub before considering longer use.

### What is the difference between a proxy service and a VPN?

A commercial VPN is usually an integrated app that connects directly. A China-focused proxy service provides nodes and a subscription URL for clients such as Clash, Shadowrocket, or V2RayNG. It may offer more nodes, lower prices, and more flexible routing, but requires separate client configuration.

### Are inexpensive proxy services reliable?

An inexpensive service can work as a backup or for light use, but price should not be the only criterion. Check operating history, support response, standard subscriptions, node stability, user reports, and trial availability. Be cautious with unusually low prices, mandatory annual billing, or sudden lifetime plans.

### How much data does a plan need each month?

Light web and social use often fits within 50–100 GB per month. Regular YouTube, ChatGPT, and GitHub use may need 150–300 GB. Users who stream 4K video, use many devices, or download heavily may need 500 GB or more.

### Which client does a beginner need?

Use [Clash Verge Rev](/en/article/0gematwc/) on Windows, macOS, or Linux; [Clash Meta for Android](/en/article/eh8f4n86/) or V2RayNG on Android; and [Shadowrocket](/en/article/z747kgjd/), Clash Mi, or Quantumult X on iPhone and iPad. Copy the provider's subscription URL into the client after purchase.

### How can I reduce provider-shutdown risk?

Avoid buying a long plan immediately; prefer monthly or pay-as-you-go packages. Monitor the website, announcement channel, ticket responses, and user group. Keep a backup provider, and stop renewing if support disappears, nodes remain offline, the group is muted, or the website repeatedly fails.

## How to avoid poor or risky providers in 2026

### Warning signs of a shutdown

Watch for these conditions and limit losses. Examples include [Atom](/en/article/cge8887u/), [EFCloud](/en/article/nz313xp6/), and the [Xiaoniu Cloud alert](/en/scamvpn/xiaoniu/):

- ❌ support does not reply for more than three days;
- ❌ the Telegram group is dissolved or muted;
- ❌ subscription URLs repeatedly fail;
- ❌ many users report that the service is unusable;
- ❌ the website remains inaccessible for an extended period;
- ❌ the operator suddenly sells an unusually cheap “lifetime” plan.

### Practical selection rules

- ✅ **Operating history:** Prefer at least two years.
- ✅ **User community:** Look for an active Telegram or QQ group, while remembering that group activity alone does not prove reliability.
- ✅ **Payment:** Confirm that a supported payment method is available to you.
- ✅ **Support response:** Look for a substantive reply within 24 hours during normal operation.
- ✅ **Plausible pricing:** Treat prices far below the cost of the advertised service—for example, below CNY 5 per 100 GB—as a risk signal, not an automatic bargain.
- ✅ **Route quality:** Verify claimed IEPL or IPLC routes rather than relying only on labels.
- ✅ **Trial availability:** Prefer a trial or clearly documented refund policy.

### Provider types that require extra caution

- ⚠️ **New providers operating for less than six months:** limited history makes shutdown risk harder to assess.
- ⚠️ **No support channel:** there is no effective path for resolving an outage or billing problem.
- ⚠️ **Frequent domain changes:** may indicate operational instability, although a domain change alone is not proof of wrongdoing.
- ⚠️ **Mandatory annual billing:** concentrates the user's financial exposure.
- ⚠️ **Aggressive use of sensitive marketing terms:** can attract unnecessary attention to the service.
