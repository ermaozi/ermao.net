The Chinese slang “airport” can be confusing in this context. It refers to a provider that sells proxy nodes through a subscription URL. This guide explains the relationship among providers, VPN apps, Clash, Shadowrocket, nodes, and subscriptions, then shows how to buy a first plan and test it safely.

No protocol expertise is required. The goal is to understand what is being purchased, how the URL is imported, how to test the service, and which warning signs should stop a renewal.

## Quick answer: a low-risk first purchase

1. Understand the roles: **the provider supplies subscriptions and nodes; Clash or Shadowrocket is the client that connects to them**.
2. Buy only a trial or monthly plan first, not an annual, multi-year, or “lifetime” plan.
3. Prefer standard subscription support, a working ticket channel, and stable nodes in the regions you need.
4. During the 8–11 p.m. peak period, test YouTube, ChatGPT, Google, GitHub, or the actual services you use.
5. Once the primary provider has proved stable, keep an inexpensive independent backup so one outage does not remove all access.

## 1. Provider, subscription, client, and node

A China-focused proxy provider supplies one or more routes and a subscription URL.

After purchasing a plan, import the URL into Clash, Clash Verge Rev, Shadowrocket, Clash Mi, V2RayNG, or another compatible client. The client downloads the available nodes—perhaps in Hong Kong, Japan, Singapore, or the United States—and lets you select one.

| Term | Role | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Provider | Sells or operates nodes and subscription access | Provider website and account panel |
| Subscription URL | Imports node and routing data into a client | Clash, standard, or V2Ray subscription |
| Client | Reads the subscription and creates the connection | Clash Verge Rev, Shadowrocket, Clash Mi |
| Node | The individual server or route carrying traffic | Hong Kong, Japan, Singapore, or U.S. node |

The provider is not itself the client. It supplies the service; the client reads its configuration and manages the connection.

## 2. Proxy subscription, VPN, Clash, and Shadowrocket

| Item | Plain-language description | Typical user |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VPN app | Usually bundles software and routes into a one-click product | Someone who does not want separate configuration |
| Proxy provider | Supplies nodes and subscription URLs for third-party clients | Someone wanting more route and client choices |
| Clash | Client that imports subscriptions and applies routing rules | Desktop, Android, or router users |
| Shadowrocket | Paid proxy client commonly used on iOS | iPhone and iPad users |

An integrated VPN may be sufficient for occasional use. A separate subscription and client provide more routing flexibility but add setup and provider-trust decisions. Neither model is automatically more private or reliable.

For a broader explanation, see [What Is Censorship Circumvention? A Clash and Proxy Guide](/en/article/fanqiang2/).

## 3. Who may benefit from a provider subscription?

Common needs include:

- reliable access to Google, YouTube, GitHub, ChatGPT, Claude, or Telegram;
- testing regional availability at Netflix, Disney+, or another streaming service;
- long-term research, software development, cross-border commerce, or international social-media work;
- using the same plan on a computer, phone, or tablet, subject to the provider's device limit;
- avoiding the need to find a new unknown free node every day.

A free subscription may work for one temporary page load. It is a poor default for important accounts or regular work because capacity, IP reputation, privacy practices, and continued availability may be unclear.

For temporary testing, see [Free Proxy Subscriptions and Their Risks](/en/article/oh8wwokl/). For regular use, consider a small monthly plan from a provider whose current operating status can be checked.

## 4. Selection table

| Need | Prioritize | Avoid as a first purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temporary research | Trial, low-cost monthly plan, or small data package | Expensive annual or lifetime plan |
| Daily ChatGPT or GitHub | Nodes in currently supported regions and actual service tests | A provider with only a few unverified regions |
| YouTube or Netflix | Stable peak-hour throughput and current streaming checks | Only off-peak speed screenshots |
| Many household devices | Standard subscriptions and adequate device allowance | A proprietary client with no export or fallback |
| Remote work | Consistently tested relay or private routes | An untested direct-only bargain plan |
| Outage backup | Non-expiring data package or small independent plan | Another service under the same operator |

There is no universally best provider. Suitability depends on the local network, required regions and services, budget, and tolerance for operational risk.

## 5. Buy the first subscription

### 1. Start monthly

Provider quality can change quickly. Buy a trial or the lowest monthly tier first.

Annual, multi-year, and “lifetime” plans concentrate risk. Be especially cautious when a service suddenly launches an implausibly cheap lifetime promotion while muting its group or ignoring tickets.

See [How to Choose a Proxy Service in 2026](/en/article/choose-good-airport/) for the full risk checklist.

### 2. Prefer an exportable, standard subscription

A Clash-compatible or standard subscription gives more freedom across Windows, macOS, Android, iOS, and routers.

A proprietary-only app is not automatically disqualifying, but verify platform support, app maintenance, export options, and the recovery path if the app stops working.

### 3. Choose regions according to need

For users in mainland China, nearby locations such as Hong Kong, Japan, Singapore, Taiwan, or South Korea often have lower latency, subject to the actual route. U.S. and European nodes can be useful for a region-specific service but usually have higher physical latency.

Do not choose only by the number of listed countries. A smaller set of stable routes may be more useful than dozens of unreliable nodes.

### 4. Check support and announcements

Look for:

- an accessible website;
- clearly described plans;
- a working ticket or support channel;
- an announcement channel that explains outages;
- a community group that is not permanently muted without explanation.

Maintenance happens. The critical risk is a prolonged outage combined with silence.

### 5. Test during peak hours

Many providers perform well during the day and slow down between 8 and 11 p.m.

Test for two or three days:

- YouTube at 1080p or 4K around 9 p.m.;
- Google, GitHub, and ChatGPT login and sustained use;
- whether the intended nodes repeatedly time out;
- a non-original Netflix title if regional streaming access is required.

Only after peak-hour performance is acceptable should the service be considered for primary use.

## 6. Choose a data allowance

| Use | Approximate data | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | 50–100 GB/month | Research, social platforms, occasional video |
| Regular | 150–300 GB/month | YouTube, ChatGPT, GitHub, and everyday work |
| Medium to heavy | 500 GB/month or more | HD video, downloads, and several devices |
| Backup | Non-expiring data package | Occasional emergency access without a monthly reset |

These ranges are examples, not a universal requirement. Measure your own usage.

Also check traffic multipliers. A `2x` or `3x` node may deduct 2 or 3 GB from the plan for each 1 GB transferred. A higher multiplier on a private route can be a plan rule, but it reduces the effective allowance.

Budget-conscious users can begin around CNY 10–20 per month where such plans are available. Users who require more consistent remote work or streaming can compare IEPL, IPLC, and well-managed relay routes, but route labels must be verified. See [How to Identify a Provider's Route Type](/en/article/r50bpg9j/).

## 7. Import the subscription into Clash

The interface differs by client, but the sequence is consistent:

1. Register and purchase a plan.
2. Find **Subscription URL**, **Import**, or **Copy Clash Subscription** in the provider dashboard.
3. Open **Profiles**, **Subscriptions**, or **Configuration** in the client.
4. Paste and update the URL.
5. Open **Proxies** and select a responsive node.
6. Enable the system proxy or VPN connection.
7. Use Rule Mode so local traffic can remain direct while selected traffic uses the proxy.

Platform guides:

- Windows, macOS, Linux: [Clash Verge Rev](/en/article/0gematwc/)
- Android: [Clash Meta for Android](/en/article/eh8f4n86/)

## 8. Evaluate the service

### 1. Stable latency matters more than the lowest number

A 60 ms node that repeatedly times out is less useful than a stable 100 ms node. Compare variation and packet delivery, not only the best test result.

### 2. Peak-hour availability

Oversold providers may look fast during the day and fail at night. A primary service should at least support ordinary browsing, 1080p video, and the required AI or developer tools at peak time.

### 3. Subscription reliability

Repeatedly failed subscription URLs or constant manual replacements create operational burden. A responsible provider should maintain working URLs and communicate a domain change clearly.

### 4. Test complete workflows

- Research: Google, Wikipedia, GitHub
- Video: YouTube, Netflix, Disney+
- AI: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini
- Social: Telegram, X, Instagram

Do not stop at the landing page. Test login, playback, uploads, search, and a longer session.

### 5. Shutdown warning signs

Stop renewing when several of these appear:

- an implausibly cheap lifetime plan;
- widespread node failures with no updated announcement;
- a suddenly muted Telegram group;
- tickets unanswered for more than 48 hours;
- repeated domain changes without clear notice.

See the [2025–2026 Shutdown and Risk List](/en/scamvpn/jichang-paolu-huizong/).

## 9. Can a free provider serve as the primary connection?

The recommendation is no.

Free nodes can test whether a newly installed client works. They are unsuitable for important accounts, content production, commerce, social-media operations, or remote work that requires a stable IP and predictable availability.

Common problems:

- many users share one exit IP, increasing service risk controls;
- peak-hour capacity is poor;
- the operator and data handling may be opaque;
- the subscription can disappear without notice.

A small paid plan does not eliminate trust or shutdown risk. Limit advance payment, verify the operator's current status, and maintain an independent backup.

## 10. A beginner selection sequence

1. Open the [provider guide](/en/posts/vpn/) and shortlist a service with a standard subscription, acceptable price, and no unresolved recent risk alert.
2. Buy only a trial or monthly plan.
3. Follow the client guide for the device and import the subscription.
4. Test for two or three days, emphasizing peak hours.
5. Upgrade data only after the service has performed reliably.
6. Leave promptly if outages and support failures persist.

## 11. Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### Can I share a subscription URL?

Avoid sharing it. The URL often functions like an account credential and may let someone consume the plan's data. Providers can also enforce concurrent-device limits.

### What does it mean when every node shows an error?

The client cannot reach the nodes. Possible causes include an expired subscription, a local-network problem, an outdated client, a system-proxy conflict, a provider outage, or an inaccessible subscription domain. Refresh the profile, test another node, then read the provider notice or open a ticket.

### How do I import a subscription into Clash?

Copy the Clash-compatible or standard URL, add it under **Profiles**, **Subscriptions**, or **Configuration**, update it, select a node under **Proxies**, and enable the system proxy.

### Do I need to buy Clash?

Clash Verge Rev and Clash Meta for Android are generally distributed at no charge. Shadowrocket is a paid iOS app, while Clash Mi is a free alternative. The provider subscription—not the client executable—is normally the recurring purchase.

### Can one subscription be used on a phone and computer?

Many providers allow multiple devices, but each plan can set its own concurrent-device limit. Importing the same URL on both devices is possible only within those terms.

### Why does ChatGPT open but reject login?

Possible causes include the exit IP's reputation, an unsupported region, browser state, or account security checks. Test a currently supported region, clear only the relevant site data, or compare another browser before concluding that the client is broken.

### Should I choose Hong Kong or the United States?

Nearby nodes often have lower latency for everyday browsing. Use a U.S. node when a service specifically requires that region. If a node is congested, test another node in the same region before changing countries.

### Can the subscription be used on a router?

Yes, when the router client accepts the subscription. OpenClash and similar tools can route household devices through one configuration. See the [whole-home router guide](/en/blog/softrouter/).

### Is purchasing a VPN or proxy service legal in mainland China?

This is a legal and policy question whose answer can change and depends on the facts. Use only for lawful purposes and do not resell access or operate an unauthorized service. See [VPN Purchases and Mainland China Law](/en/blog/vpnhefa/) for the source site's discussion; seek qualified legal advice for a real decision.
