## Why have so many proxy services shut down recently?

Some operators may have intended to take users' money and disappear, but the broader operating environment has also become much harsher. Many recent outages have followed upstream enforcement and infrastructure-removal notices.

For more context, read [April 2026 Proxy-Service Outages: Enforcement Notices, Disconnected Lines, Price Pressure, and What Users Can Do](/en/blog/april-airport-outage-2026/).

In short, **many mainland China data centers that supply relay routes have been conducting large-scale inspections and removals**. The domestic entry nodes on which many providers depend have consequently been disconnected. If rebuilding the routes is too expensive, or the operator cannot obtain replacement data-center resources, one possible outcome is that the operator **takes the remaining funds, closes the service, and disappears**.

Under the current enforcement pressure, **more proxy services may be unable to continue operating and may shut down**. That is why this monitoring page exists.

A service approaching failure often passes through three stages:

1. **Many nodes stop connecting**, often as infrastructure is being removed.
2. **Customer support stops replying**, after the operator cannot restore the service.
3. **The website becomes inaccessible or the group is dissolved**, and the operator disappears after collecting the last payments.

::: tip Core advice
If **all three conditions occur together**, the service is unlikely to recover. Stop automatic renewal and switch to a backup provider.
:::

## Common warning signs before a shutdown

An operator preparing to disappear often leaves warning signs. Watch for these **five high-risk signals**:

* \[x] **Widespread outages are not repaired:** many nodes suddenly fail and remain unattended for days.
* \[x] **Support stops responding:** staff who once replied quickly no longer answer tickets or direct messages.
* \[x] **The Telegram group or channel becomes inactive:** announcements stop, or posts become unrelated to the service.
* \[x] **The website is unreliable but checkout works:** the main website repeatedly fails while the plan-purchase page remains unusually reliable.
* \[x] **Existing users cannot connect while new users can still buy:** current subscriptions no longer work, but the website continues aggressively selling plans.

## How to limit potential losses

No proxy service is risk-free. The key is to limit exposure:

::: info Three rules for reducing risk

1. **Avoid annual plans; prefer monthly billing:** Buy your primary service monthly, or at most quarterly. If it fails, the loss remains limited.
2. **Always keep a pay-as-you-go backup:** Maintain an inexpensive data package with no short expiration. It can take over immediately when the primary service fails.
3. **Leave when the warning signs accumulate:** If the group is full of outage reports or all five signals above appear, stop renewing instead of hoping the situation will improve.
   :::
