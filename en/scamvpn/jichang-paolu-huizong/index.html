⚠️ 2025–2026 Proxy-Service Shutdown and Risk List

This page lists proxy services that shut down, or showed a high likelihood of shutting down, during 2025 and 2026. It is compiled from this site's tracking records as a quick reference for avoiding known risks.

Overview: As operating conditions have tightened and data centers have more strictly removed proxy infrastructure, many services have recently gone offline or lost contact with users. This page continuously tracks services that shut down or showed extremely high risk in 2025–2026. It includes the date each case was identified, signs of service failure, and links to the evidence page. Check this list before purchasing or renewing any service.

Important

This list is intended to help readers avoid known risks. Inclusion is not a recommendation. Every proxy service can fail, so keep a backup and do not rush into an annual plan.

Why have so many proxy services shut down recently?

Some operators may have intended to take users' money and disappear, but the broader operating environment has also become much harsher. Many recent outages have followed upstream enforcement and infrastructure-removal notices.

For more context, read April 2026 Proxy-Service Outages: Enforcement Notices, Disconnected Lines, Price Pressure, and What Users Can Do.

In short, many mainland China data centers that supply relay routes have been conducting large-scale inspections and removals. The domestic entry nodes on which many providers depend have consequently been disconnected. If rebuilding the routes is too expensive, or the operator cannot obtain replacement data-center resources, one possible outcome is that the operator takes the remaining funds, closes the service, and disappears.

Under the current enforcement pressure, more proxy services may be unable to continue operating and may shut down. That is why this monitoring page exists.

A service approaching failure often passes through three stages:

  1. Many nodes stop connecting, often as infrastructure is being removed.
  2. Customer support stops replying, after the operator cannot restore the service.
  3. The website becomes inaccessible or the group is dissolved, and the operator disappears after collecting the last payments.

Core advice

If all three conditions occur together, the service is unlikely to recover. Stop automatic renewal and switch to a backup provider.

Shutdown and high-risk list

The list below is ordered by the time the problems began.

  • Alert means the service has serious problems—for example, unreachable support or many failed nodes—but has not yet fully ceased operating.
  • High Risk means the service already shows the conditions associated with a shutdown: widespread node failures and completely unreachable support, while the website may still accept payments.
  • Shut Down means the service has ceased operating: the website is down, all nodes have failed, and the operator is unreachable.

How to limit potential losses

No proxy service is risk-free. The key is to limit exposure:

Three rules for reducing risk

  1. Avoid annual plans; prefer monthly billing: Buy your primary service monthly, or at most quarterly. If it fails, the loss remains limited.
  2. Always keep a pay-as-you-go backup: Maintain an inexpensive data package with no short expiration. It can take over immediately when the primary service fails.
  3. Leave when the warning signs accumulate: If the group is full of outage reports or all five signals above appear, stop renewing instead of hoping the situation will improve.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an alert and a confirmed shutdown?

An alert means the service has serious problems—for example, support is unreachable or many nodes have failed—but it has not completely stopped. A confirmed shutdown means the website and nodes are offline and the operator is entirely unreachable.

Are the dates in the table exact?

Most dates are when this site first identified abnormal conditions or published a confirmed assessment. For services that remained barely available or kept a storefront open while operations failed, the date of final failure may differ by several days.
