⚠️ Naiyun Shutdown Confirmed—July 1, 2026

As of July 1, 2026, multiple users had reported unreachable Naiyun support, failed nodes, an inaccessible website, and invalid subscription URLs. Yinyun's official channel had also published an announcement confirming that Naiyun had closed.

Confirmed shutdown: Existing users should stop renewing or adding credit and preserve subscription, plan, payment, and communication records.

Risk-monitoring board

Many proxy services have recently become unreachable after data centers removed their servers. Consult the 2025–2026 Proxy-Service Shutdown and Risk List for additional records.

Latest Naiyun update (July 1, 2026)

On July 1, 2026, Yinyun's official channel announced that Naiyun had closed. Together with reports beginning in early June of unreachable support, failed nodes, an inaccessible website, and invalid subscription URLs, this moved Naiyun from an alert to a confirmed shutdown.

The source evidence also connects Naiyun, CAC, Yinyun, and OKAC to the same operator. Problems with websites, nodes, or communication channels across these services during the same period should therefore be evaluated as correlated risks rather than entirely independent incidents.

Risk notice

This conclusion is compiled from available screenshots, user reports, and the Yinyun official-channel announcement.

Naiyun is treated as a confirmed shutdown. New purchases and renewals are also discouraged at CAC, Yinyun, and OKAC while the connected risks remain unresolved.

Analysis of Naiyun's shutdown

Caution

Naiyun developed the combined failure of support, nodes, website access, and subscriptions, followed by confirmation of closure from an official channel under the connected operator.

Stop purchasing and renewing to avoid further losses.

Website: Users reported that it could not be accessed normally.

  • Website: On June 30, 2026, users reported that Naiyun's website was inaccessible.
  • Client and subscriptions: Concentrated reports of nodes failing to connect began on June 10, followed by invalid subscription URLs.
  • Support: Reports that customer support could not be contacted continued from June 9.
  • Confirmation: On July 1, Yinyun's official channel stated that Naiyun had closed.

July 1, 2026: Yinyun's official channel confirmed Naiyun's closure

The announcement attempted to distance Yinyun from Naiyun. The source article's assessment, supported by the communication records below, is that Naiyun, CAC, Yinyun, and OKAC share an operator. Problems across their websites, nodes, and support during the same period therefore indicate correlated risk.

From June 9, 2026: support problems

Beginning in early June, users reported that Naiyun support could not be contacted. Continued silence from support during an outage is a serious risk signal for a paid proxy service.

From June 10, 2026: node and subscription failures

Reports then expanded to nodes failing to connect and invalid subscription URLs. The problem had moved from support communication to the service's core availability.

June 28–30, 2026: overlapping website and service failures

Users were still reporting unstable or unusable nodes on June 28. By June 30, the website was inaccessible and subscription URLs were reported invalid. Naiyun was no longer experiencing a single isolated failure; several critical systems were failing at once.

Connected operator

Naiyun, CAC, Yinyun, and OKAC share an operator according to the source evidence. The CAC and OKAC website screenshots are retained here to show that connected services developed problems during the same period; users should account for all of them when limiting risk.

Conclusion

The visible evidence shows failures across support, node availability, the website, and subscriptions, with no recovery. On July 1, 2026, Yinyun's official channel also announced that Naiyun had closed.

Current classification: confirmed shutdown.

Questions that still require further evidence include:

  • whether a refund, migration, or compensation plan will be offered to Naiyun users;
  • the source and verifiable evidence for the claim that an upstream data center removed the service in mid-June;
  • whether CAC, Yinyun, or OKAC will offer any usable migration path or compensation plan.

Loss-mitigation advice

  • Stop renewing and adding credit immediately.
  • Export and back up subscription URLs, plan records, payment evidence, tickets, and conversations.
  • If a ticket or contact channel still works, submit a written refund or explanation request and preserve screenshots.
  • Move primary use to an alternative service with a separately verifiable operating status.
