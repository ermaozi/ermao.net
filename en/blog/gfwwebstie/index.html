What Can You Do After Connecting? Useful Global Websites in 2026

About 1870 wordsAbout 6 min

Internet accessGlobal websitesAI toolsDeveloper resources

...

2025-11-30

Reaching the global internet is only the first step. The more useful question is how to find reliable information, learn new skills, work with international communities, and enjoy media without giving up your privacy or account security.

This directory covers widely used AI, social, entertainment, developer, and academic services. Product availability, prices, and features vary by country and can change, so use each official link to verify the terms that apply to your account.

Access tools do not make every website legal or safe. Follow the laws that apply where you are, respect copyright and platform rules, and do not treat a proxy or VPN as anonymity.

AI and productivity

ChatGPT

  • Official site: https://chatgpt.com
  • What it does: OpenAI's general-purpose assistant for writing, analysis, coding, research, images, and other supported workflows.
  • Useful for: outlining a document, explaining unfamiliar material, reviewing code, and turning notes into a first draft.

Always verify important answers against primary sources. Do not paste passwords, API keys, private customer data, or confidential documents unless your account and organization have an approved data policy.

Gemini

  • Official site: https://gemini.google.com
  • What it does: Google's multimodal AI assistant.
  • Useful for: text and image tasks, research, and supported integrations with Google services.

Features depend on the selected model, account type, language, and region. Review the permission prompt before connecting mail, files, or workplace data.

Grok

  • Official site: https://grok.com
  • What it does: xAI's conversational assistant, with product-specific search and media features.
  • Useful for: comparing another model's answer and following material surfaced from the web or X.

Do not assume that any AI service is private by default. Read the current privacy and data-control settings for your account, and independently check cited sources.

Microsoft Copilot and GitHub Copilot

  • Microsoft Copilot: https://copilot.microsoft.com is a general assistant.
  • GitHub Copilot: https://github.com/features/copilot is designed for software-development workflows and IDEs.

The two products are related by name but are not interchangeable. For coding, review generated changes, run tests, and check license and security implications before merging.

Social networks and communities

Telegram

  • Official site: https://telegram.org
  • What it does: messaging, channels, groups, voice calls, and bots.
  • Useful for: project announcements, topic communities, and cross-device communication.

Telegram cloud chats are not end-to-end encrypted. Secret Chats provide a different security model and are device-specific. Hide your phone number where appropriate, enable two-step verification, review active sessions, and distrust unsolicited investment or account-recovery messages.

See the Telegram setup guide for a safer starting configuration.

X

  • Official site: https://x.com
  • What it does: short posts, media, live commentary, and public conversations.
  • Useful for: following official project accounts, journalists, researchers, and time-sensitive announcements.

Create Lists for separate topics and verify breaking claims outside the platform. A verified-looking name, screenshot, or high follower count is not proof of identity.

Reddit

  • Official site: https://www.reddit.com
  • What it does: topic-based communities with voting and threaded discussion.
  • Useful for: finding practical experiences, troubleshooting history, and niche interest groups.

Check each community's rules and read recent comments as well as the highest-voted answer. Popularity is not a substitute for evidence, especially for health, legal, financial, and security advice.

Instagram

  • Official site: https://www.instagram.com
  • What it does: photos, short videos, messaging, and creator profiles.
  • Useful for: photography, art, design, travel, and visual references.

Use saved collections to organize references, but check licensing before reusing an image outside the platform.

Discord

  • Official site: https://discord.com
  • What it does: text, voice, video, and community servers.
  • Useful for: open-source projects, games, developer communities, and live support.

Disable unnecessary direct messages, review connected applications, and never run a command or download a file merely because a server member claims to be support staff.

Video, streaming, and inspiration

YouTube

  • Official site: https://www.youtube.com
  • What it does: educational, documentary, entertainment, music, and live video.
  • Useful for: lectures, product demonstrations, language practice, and long-form reporting.

Use captions and playback speed for study. Availability of Premium, background playback, downloads, and individual videos depends on the country, account, and rights holder.

Netflix

  • Official site: https://www.netflix.com
  • What it does: subscription film and television streaming.
  • Useful for: Netflix originals and licensed regional catalogs.

Catalogs, plans, advertising, video quality, household rules, and audio formats vary. A title available in one country may be absent in another, and using access methods that conflict with Netflix's terms can cause playback failures.

Twitch

  • Official site: https://www.twitch.tv
  • What it does: live streams focused on games, esports, music, and creators.
  • Useful for: live events, game communities, and interactive broadcasts.

Prime Gaming benefits and channel subscriptions depend on region and current terms. Treat chat links and supposed giveaways with caution.

Pinterest

  • Official site: https://www.pinterest.com
  • What it does: visual discovery and themed collections.
  • Useful for: mood boards, design references, outfits, recipes, and home projects.

An image appearing in search or on a board does not make it free to reuse. Follow the source link and confirm the creator and license.

Development and research

GitHub

  • Official site: https://github.com
  • What it does: Git repositories, issues, releases, code review, automation, and project collaboration.
  • Useful for: reading source code, contributing to open source, and publishing a portfolio.

Search filters such as language:python stars:>1000 can narrow results, but popularity does not guarantee maintenance or safety. Review release signatures, dependencies, permissions, and recent activity before running a project.

If connectivity is unreliable, use the GitHub access troubleshooting guide.

Google Scholar

  • Official site: https://scholar.google.com
  • What it does: discovery of papers, theses, books, citations, and scholarly profiles.
  • Useful for: finding related research and exporting a preliminary citation.

Citation exports can contain incomplete or incorrect metadata. Check the publisher's record, DOI, author list, and publication status before submitting academic work.

Google Search

  • Official site: https://www.google.com
  • What it does: general web search across pages, images, news, maps, and other indexes.
  • Useful for: locating primary documentation and comparing independent sources.

Useful operators include quoted phrases, site:example.com, filetype:pdf, and a minus sign to exclude a term. Search results are ranked, not fact-checked; verify the publisher and date.

Stack Overflow

  • Official site: https://stackoverflow.com
  • What it does: question-and-answer archives for programming.
  • Useful for: understanding error messages and comparing implementation approaches.

Check the answer date, language or library version, comments, and documentation. The accepted answer may be obsolete even when it once solved the original question.

Dropbox

  • Official site: https://www.dropbox.com
  • What it does: file synchronization, sharing, and version history.
  • Useful for: cross-device work and team file exchange.

Synchronization is not automatically a complete backup: deletion, corruption, ransomware, retention limits, and account loss can propagate or make recovery impossible. Keep an independent, tested backup of important data.

Use these services efficiently and safely

Build an information-verification habit

  1. Start from an official document or primary source.
  2. Check its publication or update date.
  3. Compare important claims with a second independent source.
  4. Separate evidence from comments, ads, screenshots, and AI-generated summaries.
  5. Save a stable reference for decisions you may need to audit later.

Translate without losing the source

Browser translation and bilingual-reading extensions can make unfamiliar material accessible. Keep the original visible for legal terms, commands, product names, numbers, and technical vocabulary; machine translation can change their meaning.

Protect accounts

  • use a password manager and a unique password for every service;
  • prefer passkeys or a hardware security key where available;
  • otherwise use an authenticator app and save recovery codes offline;
  • review active sessions and connected applications periodically; and
  • never buy verification codes or rent a stranger's phone number for an important account.

Email and phone-number acceptance varies by service and risk system. A rented number can be reclaimed and may permanently prevent account recovery.

Choose network routing deliberately

  • use a nearby, stable route for interactive work;
  • expect video quality to require more sustained bandwidth than text;
  • use rule-based routing if global routing unnecessarily slows local services; and
  • avoid free endpoints that require installing unknown certificates, profiles, or software.

For provider-selection criteria, see the regularly maintained proxy-service review index.

FAQ

Why do Google or YouTube still fail to load?

Check one layer at a time:

  1. confirm that the client is connected and has a selected route;
  2. check whether system proxy or TUN mode is actually active;
  3. test another browser without conflicting proxy extensions;
  4. compare DNS resolution with direct HTTPS connectivity; and
  5. try another route only after saving the original error.

An HTTP status page, DNS answer, or client "connected" badge alone does not prove end-to-end access.

Do these services require a foreign phone number?

Not always. Requirements depend on the service, country, account history, and automated risk checks. Use your own durable recovery methods when accepted. Do not rely on disposable SMS services for an account that holds files, purchases, contacts, or identity data.

If a separate eSIM is appropriate for legitimate travel or account use, first understand its ownership and recovery model in the 9eSIM guide.

Does HTTPS or a VPN make browsing anonymous?

No. HTTPS encrypts the content exchanged with a website, but the website still sees the connection and account activity. Network operators can often infer destinations from IP addresses, DNS, timing, and traffic patterns, while a VPN or proxy operator may gain visibility into connection metadata. Cookies, browser fingerprinting, logins, payments, and shared identifiers can also link activity to you.

Use privacy settings, minimal permissions, separate profiles where appropriate, and reputable services. Do not promise yourself anonymity that the technology does not provide.

Closing note

The global internet is most valuable when it expands the quality of your sources and communities, not merely the number of sites you can open. Start with one concrete goal, keep accounts secure, verify what you read, and add services only when they provide lasting value.
