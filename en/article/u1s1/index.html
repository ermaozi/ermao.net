U1S1 Review—IPLC Claims, No Device Limit, and CNY 20 Entry Plan

U1S1 is described as a service operating since 2025. It markets IPLC-labelled routes, no limit on IP addresses or connected devices, up to 2,000 Mbps of bandwidth, and no evening-peak throttling.

  • Website used by the source: ermaozi01.vipaff.cc
  • Lowest recorded tier: CNY 20/month for 120 GB
  • One-time package: CNY 580 for 1,000 GB
  • Telegram group: none recorded
  • Service-access claims: Netflix, TikTok, Disney+, HBO, YouTube, and ChatGPT

Those route, capacity, device, and access statements are May 2026 claims requiring current checkout and local verification.

Overview and boundaries

U1S1 may be relevant to someone testing several devices or seeking a higher-data tier. It is less suitable for someone who depends on a public Telegram community: the source says support is limited to messages or tickets.

The source emphasizes common destinations in Hong Kong, the United States, Japan, South Korea, and Singapore. Users who require Latin America, Africa, or another less common region should check the current node list before paying.

Speed and stability evidence

The provider advertises up to 2,000 Mbps and says it does not reduce speed during the evening peak.

The Chinese source interprets the screenshots as showing many available nodes, hundreds of megabits or more on several locations, acceptable latency from 20:00 to 22:00, and quick YouTube 4K seeking. It does not publish the access ISP, hardware, plan, raw loss, full measurement table, repeated days, or a capacity methodology.

Green test rows are not proof that an IPLC route caused the result or that another subscriber can saturate a 500–1,000 Mbps home connection. The 2,000 Mbps figure is a maximum provider label, not a per-account guarantee.

Streaming, TikTok, and AI access

The source reports Netflix, Disney+, HBO, YouTube, TikTok, and ChatGPT access and associates the no-device-limit policy with simultaneous household streaming.

Its stronger statements—that TikTok use is unlikely to trigger risk controls and that ChatGPT should avoid CAPTCHA or datacenter-IP restrictions—are not durable guarantees. Platforms classify addresses and accounts independently. Test the exact catalog, region, feature, and account workflow needed.

Device policy

“No IP or connection-count limit” can be useful for a multi-device household, but it does not necessarily authorize unrelated-user sharing or unlimited high-intensity use. Review the current fair-use and account-security policy before team deployment.

Frequently asked questions

Can a beginner use U1S1?

Yes, but the IPLC and no-limit labels should not replace a small monthly trial. Confirm import, common nodes, peak-hour quality, and support response first.

Which plan is appropriate?

Start with 120 GB for light use. Consider a larger tier only after measuring real usage and verifying any traffic multipliers. Buy the one-time package only after the route and operator have been tested.

Does it guarantee performance during the evening peak?

No. The provider makes a no-throttling claim, while the source supplies only archived screenshots and narrative observations. Test latency, loss, jitter, and the actual application on the intended ISP.

What should be checked before purchase?

Verify real usable bandwidth, current regions, device and sharing rules, support route, and the terms of any supplied application account.

Before purchasing

  • Confirm the official domain, operator, and ticket route.
  • Verify plan, reset, multiplier, device, sharing, and refund rules.
  • Treat IPLC, 2,000 Mbps, no-throttling, and access entries as claims.
  • Begin monthly before buying a large one-time balance.
  • Test the exact ISP, evening peak, region, and application required.
